How it works

The matrix

M is 46×36. Each row is a classmate; each column is a yes/no trait question. That is everything the engine knows.

M ∈ {0,1}^{46×36}

SVD

M = UΣVT (same mathematics as numpy.linalg.svd). With k = 14 latent directions we retain on the order of 90% of variance (exact % from the live singular values in the math panel). The first component typically dominates trait space.

M ≈ U_k &Sigma_k V_k^T,   Z = M V_k

The score

Your answers form a vector a. Early rounds use raw agreement; from the 4th answer onward we blend in cosine similarity in the 14D latent space. Each next question maximises binary entropy under the current belief over classmates.

zuser = V_k a,  score ∝ cos(Z_i, zuser)

Key ideas: (1) The full matrix encodes all knowledge. (2) SVD finds 14 nearly independent trait dimensions from 36 correlated questions. (3) σ1 dominates — one direction (e.g. quiet vs loud, feature contrasts) explains nearly half the variance. (4) Questions are chosen to be closest to 50/50 for the remaining belief. (5) After three answers, the SVD projection filters noise from correlated questions.
